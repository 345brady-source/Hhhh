### Locked Decisions

#### A field only where the agreement has a place for it
- **D-01:** Every field the portal places by itself sits on something printed on the page for that purpose: a blank line (drawn rule, run of underscores, underlined gap, table cell left empty for an answer), a tick box (drawn square, box glyph, empty bracket pair used as a box), a signature line, or a form field the PDF already carries. No automatic field anywhere else. (AC-01)
- **D-02:** No automatic field covers printed words of the agreement. The only printing a field may cover is its own blank line or box. (AC-02)
- **D-03:** The invented signature, name and date block is removed completely. No code path places a field at a fixed position, a fixed fraction of a page, or on "the last page" because nothing was found. (AC-03)
- **D-04:** Text the sponsor completes before sending (bracketed placeholders such as a wiring table) takes no field. (AC-04)
- **D-05:** No two automatic fields overlap each other. (AC-05)

#### Every place gets its field
- **D-06:** Every printed tick box becomes a box the client can tick, sitting on the printed box and of its size. (AC-06)
- **D-07:** Every printed blank becomes a field the client can fill in, on its line and of its width, in each arrangement: beside its label; on the line under its label; at the end of a label that runs over two or more lines; inside a sentence; in a table cell. (AC-07)
- **D-08:** Every printed signature line becomes a signature field on that line. A line for initials becomes an initials field. (AC-08)
- **D-09:** Each field takes its name from the agreement's own complete label (the whole wrapped label, not its last line) and its kind from that label: signature, initials, date, amount, percentage, email, phone, tax number, address, free text, tick box. (AC-09)
- **D-10:** A field's box fits its place: it spans the printed blank, rests on the line, and does not reach into the line above or below. A signature box may be taller than a text box only as far as the empty space above its line allows. (AC-10)
- **D-11:** A tick box is never forced: a client can finish signing with boxes left unticked, unless the sponsor marks a box as required in the editor. (AC-11)

#### Who signs and who fills in
- **D-12:** The portal recognises separately whether the agreement has (a) a signature block for the investor and (b) a countersignature or acceptance block for the firm: both, one, or neither. (AC-12)
- **D-13:** Where a block exists, each of its lines gets its field for the right person: the investor's lines for the investor, the firm's lines (signature, name, title, dates, accepted amount) for the firm's signer. (AC-13)
- **D-14:** Where an agreement has separate blocks or sections for individuals and for entities, each investor gets only the block for their kind of account. Fields in the other block are not shown to them and never block them. (AC-14)
- **D-15:** Where the agreement has no countersignature block, nothing is placed for the firm and signing is set up with no firm step, unless the sponsor adds a firm field by hand. (AC-15)
- **D-16:** Where the agreement has no investor signature line, nothing is placed for the investor's signature, the sponsor is told in plain words, and the agreement cannot be used for onboarding until the sponsor adds an investor signature field by hand. (AC-16)
- **D-17:** A second investor signature line (joint subscriber, spouse, second authorised signatory), a witness line or a notary line gets its field on its line, optional, marked for the sponsor's review with the reason. Never dropped silently, never a required field of the wrong person. (AC-17)

#### Filled in from onboarding
- **D-18:** Fields whose answer the portal already holds (investor name, entity name, signatory name and title, address, email, phone, commitment amount, today's date at signing, the firm signer's name and title, the accepted amount) arrive filled in; everything else is left for the client. This holds for labels that wrap and for blanks under their label. (AC-18)

#### Honest about what it read
- **D-19:** After reading, the sponsor sees a short plain summary: pages read; tick boxes, blanks and signature lines found; whether an investor signature block was found and on which page; whether a countersignature block was found and on which page. (AC-19)
- **D-20:** When the agreement's text cannot be read (a scan, a damaged file, a reader failure), the portal says exactly that, places nothing by guesswork, and leaves the sponsor to place fields by hand. A failure is never silent and never looks like a normal result. (AC-20)
- **D-21:** Fields the portal is unsure about (who fills it, what kind it is) are still placed only on their printed blank, are marked for review with the reason, and must be confirmed or moved before the agreement can be used. (AC-21; keeps PLACE-02 and PLACE-03)
- **D-22:** For an agreement uploaded under an earlier version, the sponsor can have the portal read the document again from the editor. Fields the portal placed and the sponsor never touched are replaced by the new reading; fields the sponsor placed, moved or changed are kept. (AC-22)

## Summary

The page can be read exactly, without looking at pixels. Three things the reader already in use returns give a printed anchor for every kind of place: the characters with their true positions (from the drawing operators' `showText`, each glyph carrying its own width), the drawn lines and rectangles (`constructPath` with the paint operator that follows), and the PDF's own form fields (`getAnnotations`). I computed the extent of every run of underscores from the operators on six documents and compared it with a different reader (`pdftotext -bbox`): of 241 runs, 201 are whole words in the other reader and every one of those matches to within 0.01 pt; the walk costs under 1 ms a page. On the owner's example this reading yields exactly 36 underscore lines and 15 box characters; four of the lines stand between square brackets on page 13, which leaves the 47 places of D-28 and nothing else, with no page drawn. `[VERIFIED: experiment, SCRATCH/research-011/glyphs.js and reader-proto.js]`

The pixel scan the present code relies on is unsafe on real documents. On a public 85-page memorandum made by Word 2016 (no form in it at all), the present upload path placed 310 fields: 304 tick boxes on pairs of letters in running prose and the six-field invented block. On five public documents it placed 598 fields; a judge that needs no expectations found 373 of them unclean (not on a printed anchor, covering ink, inside brackets, or overlapping). `[VERIFIED: experiment, SCRATCH/research-011/current.js and oracle.py]` The public documents also show conventions the example does not have at all: a caption under its line, one rule shared by two or four captions, blanks made of underscores and a drawn rule end to end, short blanks used as tick marks, footnote and footer rules, underlined headings, a section heading at the foot of the previous page, a placeholder in brackets inside a signature block.

The preview's rules were reproduced (sandboxed frame on a second origin, the listed content security policy, own stylesheets put inline) and the portal runs under them: the example reads the same as locally (48 fields with the present code, 1.2 s). Three things break or go silent there: the font fetch is refused (signing finishes, odd characters print as "?"); with storage refused the upload fails with the browser's raw sentence; a failed save of the workspace is recorded in a flag nothing reads. `[VERIFIED: experiment, SCRATCH/research-011/preview.js]`

**Primary recommendation:** Replace pixel scanning with a reading built from characters (showText), drawn paths and form fields; make "anchor" the only thing a field can be created from; delete the invented block and every label-only placement; decide the signers from what was read, not from a tick box in the upload dialog; and judge every check with the expectation-free judge defined below, on generated fixtures, public real documents, the held-out example, and again under the preview's rules.

## How to read a page (settled by experiment)

### What each source gives, and where it fails

| Source | Gives | Fails on | Cost per page (measured, headless Chromium here) |
|--------|-------|----------|------|
| **Characters from `getOperatorList` → `showText`** (each glyph: `unicode`, `width`, `isSpace`; with the text state: font, size, text matrix, character and word spacing, horizontal scale, rise) | Every character's left and right edge and baseline in page points. Runs of underscores, box characters, bracket pairs, where a label ends and its blank begins inside one piece of text, where a "$" stands. Real font names through `page.commonObjs.get(id).name` (for example `Wingdings-Regular`, `TimesNewRomanPS-BoldMT`), so bold labels and symbol fonts are known. | Rotated text (skip it, as the present code does). Invisible text (render mode 3, what OCR writes): present but not printed. Type 3 fonts and vertical writing were not met and not tested. | Operator list 2 to 5 ms; the walk 0.1 to 1 ms. On scanned pages the operator list costs 30 to 60 ms because the picture is decoded. |
| **Drawn paths from `getOperatorList` → `constructPath` + `stroke` / `fill`** | Horizontal and vertical lines and rectangles with their thickness and colour. Both must be read. | Tells nothing about meaning: an underline of a heading, a footnote rule, a footer rule, a table border and a blank are all "a rule". Curves are ignored. Clipping is ignored. A rule drawn in white is still a rule unless its colour is checked. | Included above |
| **Form fields from `getAnnotations`** | For each Widget: `fieldType` (`Tx`, `Btn`, `Sig`, `Ch`), `fieldName`, `alternativeText` (the tooltip), `checkBox`, `radioButton`, `readOnly`, `required`, `multiLine`, `fieldValue`, `rect`. ### The recommended combination

1. **One pass over `getOperatorList` per page** producing three lists in displayed points (origin top-left): characters, horizontal and vertical segments, stroked rectangles; plus a flag when one picture covers most of the page. Start the current matrix from `viewport.transform`, so crop boxes and rotation are applied by the reader itself. `[VERIFIED for unrotated pages: experiment; rotated and cropped pages ASSUMED, see A2]`
2. **`getAnnotations`** for Widgets. Where a Widget exists it is the anchor, its type is the kind, its tooltip or name the label (D-01, and the specific idea in CONTEXT).
3. **No page is drawn for reading.** The page pictures the editor needs are drawn once, as today, by `loadPages`.
4. **A page whose content is one page-size picture is a scan**, whether or not recognised text lies over it: nothing is placed on it and the sponsor is told which pages (D-20, D-38). This is a recommendation, see Open Question 2.
5. **`getTextContent` is not needed** for placement once characters come from the operators. Keep the existing text-only use for the editor's snapping lines, or feed it from the same characters.

### The anchor, as data

```js
// One printed place. All numbers are points of the displayed page, origin top-left.
{ page, kind: 'line' | 'box' | 'cell' | 'form',
  x0, x1,            // extent of the blank (a shared rule is cut into one anchor per caption)
  y,                 // line: the height of the printed stroke; box/cell/form: y0, y1
  clear,             // empty paper above a line before the next printed thing (limits the box height, D-10)
  made: ['underscores', 'rule'],          // what it was read from, for the reason shown at review
  short: true,       // under about 36 pt: a tick mark or initials, never a free-text blank
  bracketed: true,   // stands between [ ] in the text: the sponsor's own placeholder (D-04): no field
  form: { type: 'Tx' | 'Btn' | 'Sig' | 'Ch', name, tip, check, radio, readOnly } }
```

A field carries where it came from: `f.auto = true`, `f.anchor = { kind, x0, x1, y }` and `f.placed = { x, y, w, h, type, signerRole, label, required, prefill, forType }` (what the reading wrote, for D-22). No anchor, no automatic field: this is the whole of D-01 and D-03 in one rule.

### Tying a blank to its label (from the conventions seen)

Order of evidence, strongest first. Each rule is a class seen in at least one real document.

1. A Widget's tooltip or name.
2. Text on the same printed line directly before the blank (label ends in a colon, a question mark, or "$"). The label is the whole run of lines back to the start of its paragraph or list item, joined, not its last printed line (D-09). The present rule (two lines, second in lower case, 90 characters) is what fails on pages 2 and 7 of the example.
3. A caption directly under the line (within about 2.2 line heights, overlapping it), in brackets or italics or alone on its line. One rule with two or four captions under it is cut at the captions.
4. A label on the line directly above the blank, when that line is short or bold or ends in a colon and the blank starts at its left edge. Any label, not only a known vocabulary (the present rule drops three blanks of the example for this).
5. A statement after a short blank or a box at the start of a line; a statement before a box at the end of a line (the model agreement puts the box at the right margin).
6. The words of the sentence the blank stands in.
7. A blank with no label directly under another blank of the same left edge continues it (address lines; "provide details:" followed by two lines).
8. A rule with no label, no caption, no stack and no sentence is not a blank: nothing is placed (a ruled-off paragraph, a decorative rule).

Where 3 and 4 both fit (a stack of lines with text between each pair), the present code already decides per stack by counting captions against labels; keep that idea and mark the field for review when the count ties.

### Kind (D-09) with only six field types

The portal's field types are fixed `[VERIFIED: assets/js/esign.js:36-43]` — `signature`, `initials`, `date`, `name`, `text`, `checkbox`. The `date` type means the date of signing, filled in by itself. So the eleven kinds of D-09 cannot be eleven types: carry the kind as a property on the field (`f.kind`: `signature`, `initials`, `date`, `amount`, `percentage`, `email`, `phone`, `taxId`, `address`, `text`, `checkbox`). A date that is not the signing date (the day an entity was formed, the day the firm admits the investor) is a `text` field of kind `date` that someone types; only "Date" beside a signature line is the `date` type.

### Who signs (D-12 to D-17)

- A block is a signature anchor with the lines stacked with it (name, title, date, tax number, address, email, phone, amount) up to the next heading or signature line.
- The investor block exists when at least one signature anchor is the investor's; the firm block exists when at least one is the firm's. The read summary reports the pages.
- Whose block: the present heading logic (`whoIn`, `headType`, `heads`, `governs` in `placement.js` section 6) already gives the right signer and the right kind of investor on the example and can be kept, with three additions seen in real documents: a heading that ends the previous page governs the next page's top; "OR" between two blocks separates an entity block from an individual block; a line whose label names a second person (second director, joint owner, spouse, witness, notary) is placed, optional, marked for review (D-17) instead of skipped (today `SKIP_HARD` drops it).

### What a finished box should use

- **Radius.** The tokens are `[VERIFIED: assets/css/tokens.css:69]` — `--r-card: 22px; --r-inner: 12px; --r-pill: 999px; --r-sm: 8px; --r-md: 12px; --r-lg: 12px; --rule-hair: 1px;`. A text field on a page shown 800 px wide is about 18 px tall and a tick box 10 to 14 px, so `--r-inner` (the well's radius) would turn every field into a pill. Use `--r-sm` as the upper bound and let it shrink with the box: `border-radius: min(var(--r-sm), <a third of the box height>)`. The box cannot measure itself with container units (it is its own container), so either make `.es-page` an inline-size container and express the radius in `cqw` clamped to `--r-sm`, or have the code that already writes `--w`/`--h` write the radius. `[ASSUMED: the exact formula is a design choice; the linter accepts `clamp()` and `min()` here, tested]`
- **Edge.** One even 1 px solid edge in every state; states differ by colour and tint, never by dashes: required empty = the signer colour at full strength with a slightly stronger tint; optional empty = `--es-line-strong` (the well's edge: "Inputs: wells in `--surface`, edge `--line-strong`, radius `--r-inner`" in `design.md`) with the paper as fill; filled = a faint edge and almost no tint; review = the warn colour, solid; another signer's = `--es-paper-line`, solid, soft fill.
- **Entry fields look like wells.** Paper fill (`--es-paper`), `--es-line-strong` edge, rounded, the focus ring an outline in the focus colour (`outline: 2px solid …; outline-offset: 2px`, already used).
- **Name tag and marks never on the agreement's words (D-42).** Put the "Review" pill and the required/done marks inside the box at its right end (they fit: the lock already sits inside), or in the page margin level with the field as the guide's flag does; choose the side by checking the reading's own character boxes for free space. A tick box shows only the box; its name is in the guide bar and in the box's spoken label.
- **Tint.** `color-mix(in srgb, var(--sc) N%, var(--es-paper))` as today; keep the paper tokens so dark mode does not darken the page (`.es-page` is `color-scheme: light`).
- **Brand colour.** In the signing step `--sc` is the firm's brand (`.es-doc-sign .es-f-mine { --sc: var(--paper-brand) !important; }`, line 128); check contrast of the edge for each sample firm.

### Limits the page puts on it

- The box is the printed blank: same left edge, same width, bottom edge on the line. Rounding and edge are drawn inside it; nothing may grow the box.

## Common Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: A square found in pixels is not a tick box
**What goes wrong:** 299 of 304 tick boxes on a real 85-page Word document sat on letter pairs in prose. **Why:** in that face two neighbouring letters close a square at the scan's scale. **Avoid:** a box comes only from a box character, a drawn square with nothing inside it, a bracket pair, or a form field. **Warning sign:** any tick box whose box contains a character other than its own.

### Pitfall 2: A rule under words is an underline
Thin filled rectangles under headings and emphasised words: 22 in one document, 78 in another. A rule with characters standing on it across most of its length is not a blank.

### Pitfall 3: Rules that belong to the page, not to a form
Footnote rules (about 144 pt, left margin, smaller print under them), a grey footer rule on every page (136 in one document), frames, table borders, a paragraph ruled off above and below. None is a blank. A rule becomes a blank only when a label, a caption, a stack or a sentence ties it.

### Pitfall 4: One blank, several pieces
Underscores then a drawn rule, or two rules, end to end on one height. Join pieces that touch or overlap within about 3 pt of height before anything else, or one blank gets two fields that overlap (D-05).

### Pitfall 5: One rule, several answers
A full-width rule with a caption under its left end and another under its right end, or four captions. Cut the rule at the captions.

### Pitfall 6: Caption under or label above
In a stack with text between lines, each text is the caption of the line above or the label of the line below. Decide per stack, not per line; mark for review on a tie.

### Pitfall 7: The sponsor's placeholders
Underscores between square brackets (in tables and inside firm blocks), `[__]`, bracketed instructions. No field (D-04). An unbracketed blank for the fund's name is ambiguous: place it, mark it for review, do not make it required of the investor.

### Pitfall 8: The heading is on the previous page
The entity section's heading can be the last line of the individual page. Carry the last heading of a page to the top of the next.

### Pitfall 9: A scan with recognised text
72 of 72 pages are pictures yet carry text and even reconstructed rules. Text present does not mean the page can be read. Test for a page-size picture.

### Pitfall 10: A booklet already filled in
Answers typed on the lines look like underlined words; answers added as notes are not in the page content at all. A blank with printed characters on it gets no field.

### Pitfall 11: Box characters depend on the font
U+F06F is a box only in Wingdings (and stays U+F06F when the font is substituted by OpenSymbol). One document's boxes are a symbol character the reader gives no usable code for. Keep a small table by font name and code; where a symbol-font character is not in the table, it is not a box (no guess).

### Pitfall 12: A printed currency or percent sign beside the blank
The value filled in must not repeat it ("$" then "$250,000" in the finished PDF today).

### Pitfall 13: A PDF with its own form fields stays editable after signing
Stamped values show through a transparent field, but the fields stay live. `form.flatten()` failed here. Setting the read-only flag on every Widget worked on three files and left them valid. XFA data is dropped by pdf-lib on load (it logs that).

### Pitfall 14: Coordinates
Operators are in user space; the page shown has a crop box and may be rotated; a form XObject brings its own matrix (`paintFormXObjectBegin`). Start from `viewport.transform`, multiply every `transform`, restore on `restore` and `paintFormXObjectEnd`. The present harness's `cropped` fixture and a rotated one must both pass.

### Pitfall 15: Fonts are only known after the operator list
`page.commonObjs.get(fontId)` throws until `getOperatorList()` has resolved for that page.

### Pitfall 16: The time goes into page pictures, not reading
Reading is under 10 ms a page; the upload still takes about 100 ms a page (112 pages: 13.2 s; 85 pages: 8.8 s) because every page picture is drawn before the editor opens, and today the page is drawn a second time for the scan. Dropping the scan removes a third of it. Show progress per page and yield to the browser between pages (D-24).

### Pitfall 17: Errors swallowed on the way
Four places hide a reading failure today (`pageLayout` 2378 and 2409-2411, `autoFields` 2430-2436, `analyze` 1203-1207). Each must report into the read result.

### Pitfall 18: Marks and tags on top of the agreement
The "Review" pill above a box, the corner marks at `top: -8px; right: -8px`, the tick box tag beside a box: all lie on printed words on a dense page.

### Pitfall 19: Touch targets grow boxes into their neighbours
See "Limits the page puts on it".

### Pitfall 20: The example must not leak, and must not be fitted
No label of the example in any check or fixture. Its `expected.json` lives outside the repository. Write the rules from the catalogue and the public documents; run the example last.

### Pitfall 21: "Not run" must not read as "passed"
A check that needs `REAL_AGREEMENT_DIR` or the public documents prints "NOT RUN" and exits with a code of its own that the phase gate treats as not passed.

```js
// on OPS.fill / eoFill: each 'r' at most 3 pt high and at least three times as wide is a horizontal rule (Word, Chromium)
// a stroked 'r' about square, 5 to 20 pt, with no character inside is a drawn tick box
```

### The expectation-free judge (the definition every check uses)

Input: the PDF and the automatic fields in points. The page is rendered by `pdftoppm -r 288 -gray` (4 px per point), ink is luminance under 150; words come from `pdftotext -bbox`; Widgets from pypdf. Implemented and run in `SCRATCH/research-011/oracle.py`.

- **On a printed anchor (line fields: signature, initials, date, name, text):** in the band from 5 pt above the field's bottom edge to 2.5 pt below it, across the field's own width, some pixel row has a run of ink covering at least 80% of that width, bridging gaps of up to 1 pt (underscore glyphs do not quite touch). Or the field coincides with a Widget (intersection over union at least 0.6).
- **On a printed anchor (tick box):** in the field's square grown by 2 pt, ink runs along two opposite horizontal sides and two opposite vertical sides for at least 55% of the side (a printed box); or along the two vertical sides only (a bracket pair); or along the bottom only (a short blank); or it coincides with a Widget.
- **Covers printed words:** line field: the share of ink inside the box, 0.75 pt in from the left, right and top and ending 1 pt above its own line, is over 0.4%. Tick box: over 0.4% ink in the middle 40% of the box.
- **Between brackets:** a word of the other reader that overlaps the field horizontally on the field's line matches `\[…__…\]`.
- **Overlaps another field:** two automatic fields on one page intersect by more than 0.5 pt in both directions.
- **Every text-encoded place has a field (for documents with no expectations):** each run of three or more underscores outside brackets and each box character from the other reader has an automatic field on it; drawn rules are counted and compared with a small expectations file kept beside the document, outside the repository.
- A field fails when it is not on an anchor or when any of the next four holds against it, at any confidence. On the present code this judge reports for the example: 48 of 48 on a printed line or box, 4 between brackets (page 13), 2 overlapping pairs (page 11), 40 of 48 clean; and for the Word memorandum 306 of 310 covering ink. It does not see a missed blank by itself: that is the last rule above and the expectations file.
- The 5 pt band is a setting. The present code rests a signature box 4 pt below its line; with the band at 3.5 pt the three signature boxes of pages 11 and 12 fail as well. D-10 says the box rests on the line: set the band to what the new box rule produces (bottom edge on the line, so 2 pt is enough) and keep it there.

## Open Questions

1. **Read again on agreements stored before this phase: what is "never touched"?**

### Known Threat Patterns

| Pattern | STRIDE | Standard Mitigation |
|---------|--------|---------------------|
| A crafted PDF runs script in the page through the reader's font handling (CVE-2024-4367, pdf.js before 4.2.67; 3.11.174 is in the affected range) | Elevation of privilege | Pass `isEvalSupported: false` to `getDocument`. `intake.js:73` already does (`lib.getDocument({ data: bytes, isEvalSupported: false })`); `esign.js:227` and `esign.js:2380` do not. `[CITED: github.com/advisories/GHSA-wgrm-67xf-hhpq]` |
| Text from the PDF injected into the page (labels, field names, tooltips) | Tampering | `h()` on every insertion; `clean()` on labels |
| A PDF built to exhaust the page (very many paths or glyphs) | Denial of service | Cap what is kept per page; yield between pages; the 25 MB cap |
| The confidential example or public documents leaking into the repository or into check output | Information disclosure | Folders outside the repository; the no-leak check; check logs print page and kind, never text |
| A finished fillable PDF altered through its live fields | Tampering | Read-only flag on Widgets at finishing; the signed record's hash already detects any change |
| The sponsor believes fields were read when nothing was | Repudiation (honesty) | The read result is stored on the agreement and shown; a failure can never produce the normal words |
