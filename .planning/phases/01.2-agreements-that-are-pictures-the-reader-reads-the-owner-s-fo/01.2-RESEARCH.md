## Summary

The prototype is closer on boxes than its score suggests and further away on everything that rests on words. Measured on HEAD through the real upload dialog: 235 of 241 places found, 1 stray, 0 overlaps, 24 kinds wrong, 72 owners wrong (all 51 co-investor places and all 21 third-party places), 136.1 s for 8 pages, every field marked. With recognition switched off, the prototype's own finder already matches 236 of the 237 boxes; the places it loses in the full run are lost later, by rules that let recognised words decide whether a box exists. That is the central fault: on a picture page the words must only name and assign a field, never create or remove one.

Three things were established by experiment. First, a finder built on "paper enclosed by four straight strokes" reaches all 241 places (237 boxes and the 4 short rules) with no false box on the form, gives nothing at all on the six prose-scan pages, and gives exactly the 15 tick boxes and the 32 line places of example-scan as geometry. Its one sensitive setting is the ink level, exact between 0.19 and 0.22 of the picture's paper-to-print range; the plan must test that band. Second, whole-page recognition is both too slow (time grows with the number of words: 8.7 to 23.7 s a page here) and not accurate enough on the words that decide (the tiny "initials" captions are read exactly 15 times in 32; one co-investor column heading is not read at all). Reading only the caption band of each found box at 4x with stretched contrast, and matching the result to a short list of deciding words within two letter changes, reads 26 of 28, 13 of 14 and 13 of 14 "initials" captions on three pages in 1.1 to 3.2 s a page, with no false match. Third, the prototype's reader cannot start in the hosted preview: the preview's policy refuses every `fetch`, so both language files (the compressed one and the committed text copy) are refused in 3 ms. A start that makes no request at all (the engine's worker started from one of the portal's own files, the language data shipped as a script file that worker imports) loads in 373 ms and reads correctly inside the reproduced frame.

**Primary recommendation:** Keep the offer flow, the messages and the vendored engine; replace the finder with the enclosed-paper method in its own pure module; replace whole-page recognition with caption bands and a tolerant match; make every field on a picture page come from a found box or rule and nothing else; give third-party places a role no signer holds; start the engine without any request. Five plans, in the order given under "Plans".

### The method: paper enclosed by four straight strokes

Every setting is relative to the picture. `s` is the picture's pixels per point (1.48 for the form, 2.08 for a 150 dpi scan).

1. **Levels.** Paper `P` = the most common grey in the bright half. Print `K` = the 0.5th percentile. Range `R = max(P - K, 40)`. Darkness `D = P - grey`, not below 0.
2. **Stroke evidence.** For a level stroke: `D` plus the darker of the pixels just over and under it (a one-pixel line that falls on two rows counts whole). A pixel belongs to a stroke only on its ridge: `D >= tau/3` and `D >= half` of that darker neighbour (the soft edge beside a dark frame is not a stroke). `tau = a * R`, **a = 0.20**.
3. **Strokes.** Runs of evidence `>= tau`, at least **4.5 pt** long, bridging a one-pixel gap; the same for uprights.
4. **Boxes.** Flood fill every patch of pixels that are not stroke. A patch is a box when: it does not touch the picture's edge; its core (rows and columns at least 60% in the patch) is at least **4.5 pt** each way; the core is at least **90%** patch; a stroke lies within 3 px outside each of the four sides over at least **85%** of the side, and keeps its distance (the same or one pixel more) over at least 85% of it; the corner blocks (a fifth of the shorter side, at least 2 px) are at least half paper; it is not more than 60% of the page wide and 30% high together; it holds no other box. The field's rectangle is the core grown by the stroke.
5. **Print inside** (`D >= R/2` share of the interior) is reported with each box and never decides whether it is a box.
6. **Free rules.** Level strokes not along a box's top or bottom, at most **1.5 pt** thick, at least **10 pt** long, evenly dark along their length (standard deviation at most 0.3 of the mean: a row of letters is not), with paper on both sides and clear paper for 5 pt above the middle four fifths.

Why each test is there, from the failures seen while building it: counting non-stroke print as paper let the round letters of the logo pass, so corners must be paper; a one-pixel corner test failed on a thick frame's soft corner, so the corner is a block; pale frames have ragged edge rows, so the core is trimmed; a box with a faint mark inside failed a paper-only fill, so fill counts non-stroke pixels and print inside is reported separately; treating the soft edge of dark frames as stroke shrank small boxes below the size limit, so only the ridge counts; a capital letter with a straight back passed until sides had to keep their distance and the interior had to be 4.5 pt.

### Recommended pipeline

1. Find boxes and free rules (no engine needed).
2. **A page with no box and no free rule is not recognised at all.** It gets no field and costs only the finder. This alone takes prose-scan from 138 s to the finder's time.
3. For each box and rule, cut its caption bands from the picture: over, under, before on its row, inside; for a tick box the row after it up to the next box. Merge bands that touch into strips. Add the heading zones (section 4).
4. Enlarge each strip **4x** with smoothing, stretch contrast (darkest pixel to black, 235 to white), recognise: single-line mode for a strip one line high, block mode otherwise.
5. Match each recognised word to the deciding words (section 4) allowing **2 letter changes for a word of 7 letters or more, 1 for 5 or 6, none for shorter**, letters only. A match made with a change marks the field for review with a plain reason.
6. One column, one answer: where at least half of a column's boxes have a caption matched as "initials", all have it.

Measured so far: 1.1 to 4.3 s a page on pages 4, 5, 7, 8 for the over and under bands only. **Not measured:** the whole pipeline on pages 1 and 2 (61 and 76 places, with tick-box rows and captions before boxes), and the heading zones. Page 2 by the painted-out method took 6.6 s, so page 2 is the page most likely to exceed 6 s; the plan must measure it first and has two levers: merge the bands of one row into one strip, and a second engine worker `[ASSUMED]`.

Conclusions:

1. The committed text-encoded fallback does not help. The preview does not refuse a file type; it refuses the request itself. Both language files and the fetch in `language()` are dead there.
2. A blob worker inherits the page's policy; a worker started from one of the portal's own files takes the policy sent with that file. Started that way, with the data arriving through `importScripts`, the reader needs no request and runs under the reproduced rules.
3. Whether the real host sends a policy with the portal's own script files is not known and cannot be reproduced here (01.1-RESEARCH records the same gap as its assumption A1). If it does, no WebAssembly engine can start there at all. The design must therefore hold without the engine: the finder is plain canvas arithmetic and needs nothing; when the engine cannot start, the boxes are still found, every field is placed by shape, named plainly, marked, and the message says exactly that the words could not be read (D-11, D-13). This is also what makes D-23 safe to reach.
4. The two extra files in the working experiment were answered by the test; they stand for two small files the portal would ship (a boot file and the language data as a script). No new dependency.

### Pattern 1: A box exists by its pixels alone

**What:** The finder's list of boxes is final. Recognition adds text to a box; it never removes one and never adds one.
**When to use:** Every picture page.
**Why:** The four boxes lost on the form were found by the finder and removed by word-dependent filters; the one stray was made from words and loose strokes.

### Pattern 2: Recognise where a decision is waiting

**What:** Strips round found boxes and rules, and heading zones. Nothing found means nothing recognised.
**Why:** Time follows the number of words read (about 24 ms a word).

### Pattern 3: Closed vocabulary, tolerant match

**What:** Words that decide kind or owner are matched to a short list within a small number of letter changes; a field decided by a changed match is marked.
**Why:** The smallest print is misread by one or two letters far more often than it is read exactly (12 exact, 26 within two, of 28).

### Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Fixed grey levels:** the prototype's `lum < 150000` and `lum < 238000`. Use the picture's own paper and print levels.
- **"Left of every box is the margin":** false whenever boxes stand at the right.
- **A blob worker or any `fetch` in the reader:** refused in the preview.
- **Fitting to this form's coordinates or wording:** D-14. Every rule above is stated without a position or a caption from the form.
- **Re-typing the form:** D-04.

## Common Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: The ink level drifts and boxes vanish or letters appear
**What goes wrong:** At a = 0.15 two boxes are lost and one letter shape is a box; at 0.32 six boxes are lost.
**How to avoid:** The three-level check in plan A; report paper level, print level and the count of boxes per page in a trace.
**Warning signs:** A page whose box count changes between a = 0.19 and 0.22.

### Pitfall 2: A band swallows prose
**What goes wrong:** The band under the last row of boxes takes in the paragraph below; time rises and a party word in the prose is taken as a caption.
**How to avoid:** Cut a band at a text line that runs wider than the box by more than its own width; never search a band of prose for party words.

### Pitfall 3: The scorer passes but the picture shows less than the page
**What goes wrong:** The compare pictures show about three fifths of each page.
**How to avoid:** Correct the picture step before D-21; do not show the owner a cropped page.

### Pitfall 4: Slight rotation breaks long boxes and lines
**What goes wrong:** At 0.4 degrees a line climbs one pixel every 143 pixels; a run that follows one row breaks.
**How to avoid:** Evidence already spans two rows; join runs that continue each other one pixel up or down. On example-scan the counts came out right without this; wide boxes on a rotated scan were not tested.

### Pitfall 5: The engine cannot start and the page is called unreadable
**What goes wrong:** Today a `load` failure places nothing.
**How to avoid:** Boxes do not need the engine. Place them, mark them, say the words could not be read.

### Pitfall 6: Normal PDFs load the reader
**What goes wrong:** D-15 breaks if any reader file is requested for a page with text.
**How to avoid:** Scorer part e checks it on every run.

### Known Threat Patterns

| Pattern | STRIDE | Standard Mitigation |
|---------|--------|---------------------|
| A crafted PDF with a huge page picture to exhaust memory | Denial of service | Pixel cap before drawing; strips, not whole enlarged pages |
| Recognised text carrying markup into a field name | Tampering | Escape on output; treat as plain text everywhere |
| The agreement's content leaving the browser | Information disclosure | No request is made by the reader; checked by the frame test (no outside request, `connect-src 'none'`) |
| A swapped engine file | Tampering | Files ship with the portal; licences beside them; no CDN |
