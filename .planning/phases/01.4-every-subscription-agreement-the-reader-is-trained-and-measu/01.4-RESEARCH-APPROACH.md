## Summary

The portal today has **no server that the portal itself runs**. All reading happens in the browser (`anchors.js` reads geometry from the PDF's drawing operators, `picturepage.js` finds boxes and rules from pixels, `textread.js` uses the vendored text-recognition engine to read the words around the places on picture pages, and `placement.js` turns places into fields with kind, person and label using rules). The only server in the repo is `server/`. It is a dependency-free Node 18+ "integration server" for identity and screening checks, and the portal treats it as **the sponsor's** server: `integrations.js:12` says "a connector on the sponsor's integration server (server/)", and `PROJECT.md:58` says "Data currently lives in the browser (localStorage and IndexedDB), with an optional integration server." D-13 therefore needs a new piece: a **reading service operated by the portal**. The existing reader code is written to run outside a browser too (the header of `anchors.js` says "This file works on plain data only … so it can also be loaded outside a browser", and `placement.js` says the same), so it can move to that service largely as it is.

No single published approach meets the owner's target alone:
- **Rules on PDF geometry** (what exists) place fields exactly where a line, box, underscore run, cell or form widget is printed. They are the best at *where*, but they meet an unbounded long tail of wording for *who completes it* and *what it asks for*.
- **Open form-field detectors** (FFDNet / CommonForms, 3 classes) find places on page images, including scans and edgeless boxes, at a reported 81.0 AP for the larger model. That is a strong prior but far from "no field to fix", and they say nothing about person or label.
- **Vision-language models** (open-weight Qwen3-VL, Apache-2.0, self-hosted on a GPU; or hosted frontier models) are the only component that can read a page the way a paralegal does: "this block is the co-investor's signature, this is the firm's acceptance, this is the entity's title line". The published evidence says they are weak at precise box localisation, so they must not be the source of geometry.

**Primary recommendation:** build a **portal reading service** (Node, beside `server/`) that runs a **three-layer pipeline**:
1. **Places** come from geometry first: the existing `anchors.js`/`picturepage.js`, unchanged in spirit. They are joined by a **form-field detector** (RF-DETR, Apache-2.0, fine-tuned on the EDGAR set plus the CommonForms data) only where geometry is blind (picture pages, edgeless tinted boxes). Every detector box is snapped to pixel evidence before it counts.
2. **Meaning** (keep or drop, person, kind, label, investor-type branch) comes from a **vision-language adjudicator** that sees the rendered page with numbered candidate marks and the PDF's own words, and answers in a strict JSON schema. It may only classify or reject numbered candidates. It never draws a field.
3. **Rules stay as the guard and the tie-breaker**: no field over printed words, a field fits its line or box exactly, a signature block is complete, a page is consistent, and any adjudicator answer that breaks a rule is overridden or sent to review.

For the adjudicator, the owner must choose between **(a)** a self-hosted open model (Qwen3-VL) on a rented GPU server, where no document leaves the portal's servers, at roughly $200 to $800 a month fixed, and **(b)** a hosted model API approved by name (for example Anthropic's Claude API under zero data retention), at roughly $0.01 to $0.30 a document and no GPU. Research recommends building the adjudicator behind one interface and **measuring both on the 40 answer sheets before the owner chooses**. The hosted option is expected to be more accurate (`[ASSUMED]`), and the self-hosted option keeps every document on the portal's own servers.

**Conclusion for Q1:** the only off-the-shelf detector that targets exactly "fill-in field, tick box, signature place" is the CommonForms family. Its classes match the portal's kinds only coarsely (TextBox covers amount, date, text, initials and title). It gives no person and no label. Its training data is born-digital fillable forms, not browser prints of EDGAR exhibits or edgeless grey boxes. **Recommendation:** train our own RF-DETR (Apache-2.0) detector, starting from FFDetr weights if their licence permits and otherwise from RF-DETR's COCO checkpoint. Train on CommonForms data (its licence must be checked: `[ASSUMED]` usable) plus EDGAR pages labelled almost for free from the vector geometry (see Q6). Use it **only where geometry is blind**.

### 2. Self-hosted vision-language models

- **Can they assign person, kind and label from page plus candidate boxes?** That is the task VLMs are built for: reading a page, understanding the headings "Signature of Co-Investor", "Accepted by the Fund" and "If an entity: Title", and attaching them to nearby marks. Published evidence that they *localise* checkboxes poorly (one vendor comparison: a YOLOv8 checkbox detector at F1 0.88 against GPT-4 Vision at 0.63; CheckboxQA reports that VLMs struggle with checkable content) `[CITED: search summaries; LOW confidence]` is the reason the design never lets the VLM choose geometry. It **classifies numbered marks** ("set-of-marks" prompting) instead. Accuracy on this task: **no published number; `[ASSUMED]` good enough with 8B to 32B models, to be measured.**
- **Model:** Qwen3-VL family, Apache-2.0 `[CITED]`, flagship DocVQA 96.5 and OCRBench 875 (vendor-reported) `[CITED]`. Start with **Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct**. Escalate to 30B-A3B (MoE, about 3B active, fast) or 32B if the 40 answer sheets show person or label errors.
- **Hardware:** 8B at FP16 is about 19 GB VRAM, INT8 about 9.6 GB, INT4 about 4.8 GB (estimate) `[CITED: Spheron GPU recommender; estimate]`. A 24 GB card (L4, RTX 4000 Ada 20 GB with INT8, RTX 4090, A10G) runs 8B; 30B-A3B needs about 24 GB at 4-bit or 48 GB at 8-bit `[ASSUMED]`. CPU-only inference of an 8B VLM takes minutes per page: **not viable** `[ASSUMED]`.
- **Speed:** no pages-per-second figure published; 73 tokens/s for a single 8B request on a 16 GB consumer card `[CITED: community NVFP4 test]`. Per page: about 1,500 to 2,500 image tokens prefill plus about 300 output tokens, so roughly 2 to 6 s per page on one 24 GB GPU; about 30 to 90 s for a 15-page signature section, and pages can be batched `[ASSUMED]`.
- **Cost:** fixed GPU rent (Q5), so the marginal cost per document is about zero.

### 3. Hosted model APIs (owner must approve by name, D-15)

- **Which would do this well:** frontier multimodal models (Anthropic Claude Opus/Sonnet 5.5, Google Gemini, OpenAI GPT) read legal pages and follow a strict JSON schema reliably `[ASSUMED]`. With the page image, the PDF's own words and numbered marks, they are expected to beat an 8B open model on person and label for unusual layouts `[ASSUMED, to be measured]`.
- **Data terms (Anthropic, cited):** no training on commercial API content; default 30-day retention; **ZDR on request, per organisation**; under ZDR, use the Messages API with base64 pages only (no Files API, Batch or prompt caching, which may fall outside ZDR); Fable-class models are not ZDR-eligible `[CITED: platform.claude.com ZDR page]`. Other providers: `[ASSUMED]`, must be confirmed.
- **Cost per document (estimate):** only pages that have candidate places go to the model, typically 6 to 15 pages of an agreement. At about 2,500 input tokens per page (page image plus words plus mark list) and about 400 output tokens per page, 12 pages make about 30k input and 5k output. **Sonnet 5.5: about $0.06 + $0.05 = about $0.11; Opus 5.5: about $0.22; Haiku 5.5: about $0.006.** Token counts are `[ASSUMED]`; prices `[CITED: skill table]`. At 1,000 agreements a month that is about $6 to $220 a month, with no GPU.
- **Latency:** pages can be sent in parallel, so 10 to 30 s for a document `[ASSUMED]`.

### 4. Combined design: which approach does which piece

| Piece | Best done by | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Exact position of a field on a vector page (line, underscore run, box glyph, drawn square, ruled empty cell, AcroForm widget) | **Rules on PDF geometry** (existing `anchors.js`) | Exact to the point; a model can only approximate. Proved on the owner's documents (D-06) |
| "Is this line a place, or an underline, footnote rule or table frame?" | **Rules first, VLM as tie-breaker** for the cases the rules mark as low confidence | Rules handle the common shapes; the VLM resolves the odd ones by reading the page |
| Places on picture pages that have drawn edges | **Pixels** (existing `picturepage.js`) | Already finds boxes and rules from pixels, with no model |
| Places with **no drawn edges** (white boxes on grey, D-05), faint scans, places that are only whitespace after a label | **Detector**, snapped to pixel evidence (a tint change, the paper inside) | Geometry cannot see them; a learned detector can |
| Pages printed in landscape with each form page split across two PDF pages | **Rules**: detect the split (a picture cut at the page edge) and stitch the halves into one virtual page before anything else runs | Deterministic; models trained on whole pages fail on half pages `[ASSUMED]` |
| Kind (signature, initials, date, amount, tick, text) | **VLM**, with rules as a guard (a small square is a tick; a line under "Date" is a date) | Wording varies without bound; rules are a cheap sanity check |
| Person (investor, co-investor, firm, custodian or other party) | **VLM** reading the block headings and the page's flow; rules enforce consistency (one block is one person; the firm's acceptance block is never the investor's) | The largest source of "fields the sponsor must fix" on unseen layouts `[ASSUMED]` |
| Label text | **VLM** proposes; `placement.isPlainLabel` normalises to the standard words | Keeps the portal's established labels |
| Investor-type branches ("Individuals sign here / Entities sign here") giving `forType` | **VLM** | Pure reading comprehension |
| Never a field where nothing is printed; never a field over words; every printed place gets a field | **Rules** (final guard, D-07) | Must be deterministic and provable |

**Where rules beat models:** geometry, exactness, guarantees, speed, explainability, and anything measured "no field over text". **Where models beat rules:** reading meaning in wording no one has seen before, edgeless or degraded places, and whole-page context (which block belongs to whom).

### 5. Infrastructure

**Who runs it (owner decision):** a central **portal reading service** operated by the portal owner. Bundling it into each firm's integration server would put a GPU (option a) or an API key (option b) on every firm's machine, which does not fit "the portal does all of it".

**Shape:**
- **Language:** Node (same code as the browser reader). The VLM runs either as a vLLM process on the GPU host, reached over private HTTP, or as the hosted API called from Node.
- **API:**
  - `POST /v1/read` with `Content-Type: application/pdf` and an `Authorization: Bearer` workspace token. The response is `202 { id }`.
  - `GET /v1/read/:id` returns `{ status: 'queued'|'reading'|'done'|'failed', progress: { page, pages }, result? }`. The result is `{ fields, pages, signatures, … }` in **exactly the shape `placement.read` returns today**, so the field editor and the save gates are unchanged.
  - `DELETE /v1/read/:id` deletes the job early.
  - The browser polls every 1 to 2 s (or uses server-sent events) while showing "Reading your agreement".
- **Queue:** in process. One worker per GPU, a few CPU workers for geometry and the detector. Jobs live in the existing store pattern (`server/lib/store.mjs` / `vault.mjs`). No Redis at this scale `[ASSUMED adequate below about 10 reads a minute]`.
- **Protection:** TLS only; the PDF is sealed with `DATA_KEY` at rest (`server/README.md:38`: "Encrypts invitations, submissions, documents and the outbox at rest"); page images are kept only in memory; **the PDF and every derived image are deleted when the job finishes**; the result is deleted after it is fetched or after a short TTL (for example 1 hour); no document text in logs; size and page limits (for example 50 MB, 300 pages); a PDF parse timeout; the job runs in a worker thread with a memory limit; per-workspace rate limit (`RATE_LIMIT_PER_MIN` already exists).
- **Hosting and monthly cost (ranges, `[ASSUMED]` unless cited):**

| Setup | What | Monthly |
|---|---|---|
| Service only, with option (b) hosted adjudicator | 1 VPS, 4 vCPU / 8 GB (geometry, detector on CPU, text engine) | $20 to $60, plus about $0.01 to $0.25 per document to the named provider |
| Service plus option (a) self-hosted, dedicated GPU | the VPS plus a dedicated GPU server (for example Hetzner GEX44 RTX 4000 SFF Ada 20 GB was €184/month plus €79 setup; it may be discontinued for GEX45 `[CITED: Hetzner press release, whtop, centron]`) | about $220 to $350 |
| Service plus option (a) on a cloud GPU, always on | AWS g5.xlarge (A10G) about $1.006/h on demand, about $734/month `[CITED: search summary]`; g6 (L4) about $0.80/h `[LOW]` | $600 to $800 |
| Option (a) on a GPU marketplace, billed by the hour | RTX 4000 Ada about $0.18 to $0.42/h `[CITED: getdeploying tracker]` | $130 to $300, **but the marketplace host is itself a third party holding documents** and must be named by the owner |

Every hosting company (the VPS, the GPU host) is also a party the documents pass through. The owner should name the host as well as any model provider.

### 6. Training and tuning on the EDGAR set

**Split, decided before anything is trained:** about 220 documents. Keep the **40 answer-sheet documents plus D-05's file as a held-out test set that is never trained on, never prompt-tuned on, and never used to write a rule**. Use set-b (110, complete) as train and dev. Use set-a, which comes from a different builder and different offering types, as a second held-out set when it is complete. This is the guard against "training it to read four files" (D-03).

**Detector labels, nearly free:**
1. On vector pages, run the existing reader and `measure.py candidates` (independent geometry). Where both agree, the place's box is exact, so that is a label. Where they disagree, a human checks the page in a small labelling page that shows the page with boxes and lets you accept, delete, add or resize. Expect about 1 to 2 minutes per page `[ASSUMED]`.
2. **Synthetic picture pages from vector truth:** render each labelled vector page as an image and degrade it in the ways EDGAR prints and scans come: grey backgrounds with white boxes (remove the box strokes), JPEG and GIF artefacts, 150 to 300 dpi, slight rotation, blur, landscape printing with the page split across two halves, browser header and footer. The labels stay exact. **This is the main lever for D-05-type pages.**
3. The 5 or more real picture-printed documents and the scanned ones: label by hand (detector pre-labels plus correction).
- **How many:** CommonForms pre-training gives the generic prior. For domain adaptation, a few hundred to about 2,000 labelled pages is typical for RF-DETR or YOLO fine-tunes `[ASSUMED]`. The set gives about 220 × 8 pages with places, about 1,700 real pages, multiplied by augmentation. Training cost: CommonForms reports under about $500 per model from scratch `[CITED: arXiv abstract via search]`; a fine-tune is a few GPU-hours `[ASSUMED]`.
- **Expected gain:** no published number for this domain. Expectation `[ASSUMED]`: on vector pages the detector adds little, because geometry is already exact. On picture and edgeless pages it should take recall from "the reader cannot read it" (D-05 today) to most places found. The measure decides.

**Adjudicator tuning:** start with **no fine-tuning**: a written page-reading guide in the prompt, a strict JSON schema, and 2 or 3 worked examples drawn from train documents only. If self-hosted Qwen3-VL falls short on the dev set, LoRA-fine-tune it on about 500 to 1,000 (page, marks, answers) examples. Make them from the reader's output on train documents, corrected through the same labelling page (`[ASSUMED]` size). Hosted models are not fine-tuned; only the prompt changes, and every prompt change is re-measured on all of dev.

### Pattern 1: Set-of-marks adjudication (the VLM classifies, never draws)
**What:** draw every candidate place as a numbered outline on the rendered page image. Send the image, the page's words with positions (from the PDF text layer or the text engine), and the mark list. The VLM returns one record per mark. A mark it drops is removed only if rules agree it is not a fill-in place; otherwise it is kept and marked. The VLM may also report "a place with no mark near words X". That report triggers a targeted geometry and detector search in that region and is never turned into a field directly (D-07).
**When:** every page that has candidates.
**Schema skeleton (the values are the portal's existing vocabulary, quoted from `placement.js:19-27`: `confidence 'high' | 'medium' | 'low'`, `forType 'person' | 'entity'`, other parties `Custodian, Broker-dealer, Representative, Adviser, Manager, Transfer agent, Third party`):**
```json
{ "marks": [ { "id": 7,
    "place": true,
    "person": "investor | co-investor | firm | other",
    "other": "Custodian | Broker-dealer | Representative | Adviser | Manager | Transfer agent | Third party | null",
    "kind": "signature | initials | date | amount | tick | text",
    "label": "Title",
    "forType": "person | entity | null",
    "sure": "high | medium | low" } ],
  "unmarked": [ { "near": "words the place follows", "why": "…" } ] }
```
`person` and `kind` value spellings must be mapped to the existing field properties in `placement.js` (which encode person through the signer and `second`/`other`). The planner reads those definitions; the strings above for person and kind are `[ASSUMED]` names for the adjudicator's own schema.

### Pattern 2: One reader, two hosts
Keep `anchors.js`, `picturepage.js`, `placement.js` and `textread.js` as the single source. The service loads them under Node, the same way the checks already do. The browser path stays for offline checks and for the D-06 regressions. Do not fork the code.

### Pattern 3: Adjudicator behind one interface
`adjudicate(pageImage, words, marks) → marks[]`, with implementations `local` (vLLM HTTP) and `hosted:<provider>`. Both are measured on the same set. The owner's choice becomes a configuration value, not a rewrite.

### Anti-Patterns to Avoid
- **Letting the VLM output coordinates that become fields.** VLM boxes are approximate; strays and fields off the line are D-04 failures.
- **Trusting a detector box without pixel evidence.** Snap it to the tint edge or the paper inside the box, or reject it.
- **Training, prompt-tuning or writing rules against the 40 answer-sheet documents.** That leaks the test set and repeats the "four files" failure.
- **Sending whole agreements to a model.** Send only pages with candidates. That costs less and exposes fewer personal details.
- **Using AGPL components (Ultralytics, PyMuPDF) or non-commercial weights (LayoutLMv3) in the product.**
- **Review marks as a safety valve.** Under D-14 every mark is a failure. A design that "marks when unsure" scores badly by construction; uncertainty has to be resolved by the second opinion (rules plus VLM plus detector agreeing).

## Don't Hand-Roll

| Problem | Don't build | Use instead | Why |
|---------|-------------|-------------|-----|
| Object detector for places on images | A custom CNN | RF-DETR (Apache-2.0), fine-tuned | Detection training has many traps (anchors, NMS, augmentation) |
| VLM serving | A custom inference server | vLLM (OpenAI-compatible) | Batching, paged KV cache, quantisation |
| PDF parsing on the server | A new parser | pdf.js 3.11.174, the same as the browser | Identical geometry to what the reader was proved on |
| Model runtime in Node | Bindings of our own | onnxruntime-node | Maintained, MIT |
| Encryption at rest | New crypto | the existing `server/lib/crypto.mjs` / `vault.mjs` with `DATA_KEY` | Already reviewed in this repo |
| Labelling tool | A large app | a small single-page labeller in scratch (boxes over the page: accept, delete, add) | Only 3 actions are needed; Label Studio would also work `[ASSUMED licence]` |

**Key insight:** the hard, valuable part is not any one model. It is the **agreement between independent signals** (vector geometry, pixels, detector, reader of words, rules), plus a measure that counts every field the sponsor would touch.

## Common Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: Geometry mismatch between browser and server
**What goes wrong:** the service uses a different pdf.js version or renderer, and boxes shift.
**How to avoid:** pin pdfjs-dist 3.11.174, and make the D-06 regressions run against the *server* output.
**Warning signs:** the owner's documents lose points (29/29, 2/2, 138/138, 241/241).

### Pitfall 2: The detector fires on label text, not the box
"Mind the Gaps" reports detectors that concentrate on printed label text `[CITED]`. **How to avoid:** use pixel snapping, the no-field-over-text guard, and the augmented training data with edgeless boxes.

### Pitfall 3: Half pages from landscape browser prints (D-05)
**How to avoid:** stitch the two halves first, using a picture clipped at the page edge that continues on the next page, and map coordinates back to each half for the final fields.

### Pitfall 4: VLM person confusion between repeated blocks
Agreements repeat the same signature block for individual, joint and entity investors and for the firm. **How to avoid:** send the whole signature section in reading order (or page plus previous heading context), keep a block-consistency rule, and score "wrong person" separately in the measure.

### Pitfall 5: Hidden third parties
The GPU marketplace, the cloud host and the API provider each receive documents. **How to avoid:** the owner names every party; there are no default keys; the service refuses a hosted adjudicator unless its named configuration is set.

### Pitfall 6: Test-set leakage
**How to avoid:** a fixed held-out list in scratch, checked by the measure (it refuses to report "test" numbers if a held-out id appears in a training manifest).

### Pitfall 7: Non-deterministic adjudicator answers
**How to avoid:** use temperature 0 or the provider's deterministic settings, a strict schema, and cache answers by page hash plus marks hash plus model version for measurement runs, so a build's score changes only when code or model change.
