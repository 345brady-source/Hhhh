## The feature vector (FEATURES_V 1, 674 names)

- **geo (5):** x, y, w and h as page fractions; the page index as a fraction of the document.
- **ev (8) and src (2):** the evidence the place stands on:
  - rule; underscore (underscores, leaders, hyphen runs); box (drawn square, bars, brackets);
  - glyph (a box character, or a tick place with no drawn box); cell;
  - comb (3 or more boxes of one size, at most 4 pt apart); dateparts (a slash between this place and one within 14 pt beside it);
  - picture (the page was read from its picture).
  - `src:model` and `src:pModel` are filled when the matched rule or box came from the place model.
- **dec (33):** the reader's own decision:
  - kind one-hot over 20 kinds plus unknown;
  - role (investor, firm or none), second, other;
  - forType (person or entity), confidence (high, medium or low), required, prefill.
- **Word slots (6 × 103 = 618):** cap (the words before it on its line), after, under (within 14 pt), over (within 20 pt), head (its block's heading), blk (its block's words). Each slot has:
  - a word count;
  - 38 keyword-family counts. 10 come from NS.pictureWords: PARTY_WORDS co, other, investor, bareCo and bareOther; KIND_WORDS initials, signature and date; the CO_INVESTOR and OTHER_PARTY patterns. The other 28 are fixed: date, amount, percent, email, phone, tax id, address, name, title, initials, signature, by, its, accepted, joint, spouse, co, custodian, trustee, adviser, broker, representative, branch manager, notary, witness, for entities, for individuals, firm;
  - 64 hashed bigram buckets.

  Words read from pictures are compared with NS.picturepage.near. A word written in two parts ("co subscriber") also matches the one-word form ("cosubscriber").
- **blk (8):** the place's index in its block, that index as a fraction, the places in the block, the signature places in the block, and the block kind one-hot (signature, section, branch, none).

**Normalisation (`norm`):** NFC, then lower case, then bracketed remarks dropped, then every run of characters other than letters and digits becomes one space, then trimmed. "Name of Subscriber (please print):" becomes "name of subscriber". In the word slots only, %, $, @ and # become the words percent, dollars, at and number before normalising.

**Hash:** FNV-1a 32-bit over the UTF-16 code units of "first second" (the normalised word pair), with offset basis 2166136261, prime 16777619 and unsigned arithmetic. The bucket is the hash modulo 64. Check value: fnv1a("a") = 0xe40c292c.

**Reading order:** rows are sorted by page, then top, then left, then the proposal's index in res.fields. `pid` is that index. `box` is [x0, y0, x1, y1] in page points.

## Block rules

- **Lines:** text runs on one baseline (within half the smaller height), read left to right.
- **Heading:** a line of at most 10 words and 80 characters that is set apart in one of three ways: capitals (80 % of at least 4 letters), 1.2 times the page's median line height, or 12 pt of clear paper above it.
  - Never a heading: a line with a place on it (rule, underscores, box), a signature caption, or a line ending in a colon or a full stop (unless it is in capitals).
- **Signature caption:** at most 8 words saying signature, signed or sign here (but not "signature page"), or a line opening with "by".
- **Branch:** a line of at most 12 words saying "if/for (an) individual, entity, trust, corporation, partnership, company, LLC, IRA or natural person", or "joint". It must be neither a caption nor a line with a place on it.
- **How blocks open and close:**
  - A branch line opens a branch block. It runs to the next heading or branch line.
  - A heading opens a section block.
  - A signature caption turns the open section block into a signature block when the block's heading is at most 4 lines above. Otherwise the caption opens a signature block with no heading.
  - A signature block ends at the next heading or branch line, or at 24 pt of clear paper. A drawn rule between two lines counts as print.
- **Which block a field is in:** the block whose span holds the field's bottom edge. A block's span runs to the next block's top.
