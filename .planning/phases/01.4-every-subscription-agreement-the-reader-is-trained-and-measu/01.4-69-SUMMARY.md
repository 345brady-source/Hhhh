# Phase 01.4 Plan 69: Every upload read with the same bar (pictures, broken text, long documents), no stutter, no question Summary

Pages that are pictures, or whose text layer cannot be read, now go to the reading server as page pictures. On each picture the portal has outlined and numbered every place it found, and each field lands on that pixel geometry. Long agreements are read whole in page ranges and the answers merged. Transient failures are retried silently in both readers and on the server. After every reading, the firm always has somewhere to sign, so saving no longer stalls. The intake now judges readability page by page and sends the whole text on the server path. The last "allow" wording is gone.

**Nothing was spent and nothing was measured on a document.** Every run used fake readers or fake intakes, with no key. SCRATCH/blind/ was never opened, listed or used. This is not ready to call working: plan 65 measures the API reader on the blind documents, and then the owner checks it.

## Picture-page rule and image size

**Which pages.** A page is a picture page when either:
- it was read from its picture (no text, `pg.pictured`), or
- its text fails `NS.intake.unreadablePages`: no letters, or under UNREADABLE_BELOW 0.1 and fewer than half of its tokens are the document's own words.

Scanned or failed pages that have no candidates are not sent.

**The picture.** pdf.js renders the page on a canvas:
- The long side is 2000 px. That is under the 3000 px limit that applies to every image when a request has more than 20.
- The page is encoded as JPEG at quality 0.85.
- Every candidate on the page is outlined in red and labelled `#id`. The label sits inside the place when it fits, so it covers no printed word; otherwise it is small and just above the place.

I looked at the pictures for the first agreement and the owner's form. The captions stay readable and every candidate carries its number.

**The text.** For a picture page, the list's text keeps the page header, then `(read the page image)`, then each line's `[#id]` tokens only, with no words. Every other page's text is byte-identical to before.

**The request.**
- The browser sends `{ pdf, pages, text, list, images: [{ page, data, marks }] }`.
- The server sends the document block first. Then, for each image, a text block `Page N with numbered candidates` followed by `{ type: 'image', source: { type: 'base64', media_type, data } }`. The list's text comes last.
- With no images and no range, the request is plan 64's exactly. A test asserts this, and the check confirmed that the browser body is `{ pdf, pages, text, list }` holding the uploaded file itself.

**Validation, before any count or call.**
- 400: images not an array, bad base64, not JPEG or PNG, a page outside the range, a duplicate page, or bad marks.
- 413: an image over 10 MB of base64, or more than 600 images.
- I checked these limits against the published documentation during execution: 600 images per request for 1M-context models, 10 MB per image (base64), 8000 px maximum, stricter beyond 20 images, 32 MB per request, and 600 PDF pages.

**Placement.** fromAnswer is unchanged. A chosen candidate keeps its pixel box. A missing place on a picture page snaps only to the picture reader's pixel lines and boxes, or is dropped and counted.

## Range rule and merge

**Limits.** These are on the esign namespace so the checks can shorten them: `NS.esign.RANGE_PAGES = 600`, `RANGE_BYTES = 31 MB` (the whole encoded body), and `IMAGE_MAX = 600`.

**Building the ranges.**
- The agreement is cut into consecutive ranges of RANGE_PAGES.
- Each range is a new PDF of exactly its pages, made with pdf-lib copyPages. It carries only its own candidates, which keep the numbers they have across the whole agreement, plus its page text and its pictures.
- A range's text starts with: `Pages a to b of the agreement's M pages. The attached PDF holds only these pages ...`.
- The body adds `range: [a, b, M]`.
- The server checks the range: the list's pages must lie inside it and its numbers must be increasing. It passes `ids, first, last` to checkAnswer, so only that range's numbers are kept.

**Halving.**
- A range whose body is over RANGE_BYTES, or that has more than IMAGE_MAX pictures, is cut in two halves.
- A range the server answers with 413 is cut in two halves and sent again. The server counts tokens (free) whenever images are present or the PDF is large, and answers 413 above 900000.
- A single page that still does not fit falls back with `too-large`. That is the only size fallback left.
- Ranges with no numbered place are skipped.

**Sending and merging.**
- Ranges are sent one after another, each with its own browser limit.
- The merge is the union of chosen numbers (the first range's entry stands) plus every range's missing places, kept with their page. fromAnswer then runs once on the whole agreement.
- The 22 MB and 600-page fallback is removed.

## Retry rule

**`NS.readingFetch(url, init)`** is in intake.js, on its own namespace line, and both readers use it.
- It sends again on a network error or on status 429, 500, 502, 503, 504 or 529.
- At most 3 attempts in all, after waits of 2 s and then 6 s.
- The caller's signal ends the waiting at once. That signal is the reader's own limit (330 s for agreements, 630 s for the intake) or Stop.
- Nothing is shown while it retries.
- 400, 401, 402 and 413 are returned at once. A 413 leads only to a smaller request.

**Server.** createClient's `maxRetries` is now 3 (it was 1), with the overall limit unchanged. Tests:
- 529 twice and then the stream: read() and the intake run both succeed.
- 529 on every attempt: the call ends after 4 attempts.
- A reply that never comes: the overall limit still ends the call.

## Firm signature guarantee

- If any field is the firm's and none of the firm's fields is a signature, one firm signature field (required) is added on the page of the firm's last field.
- Placement:
  - If the page prints an acceptance or countersignature heading (`acceptanceBlock`), the field goes on the first printed line under it, within 150 pt. With no such line, it goes just under the heading.
  - With no heading, it goes directly below the last firm field, inside the 36 pt margins.
  - The field is sized through boxOn and the signature kind.
- In-page readings: firm blanks on an agreement with no firm signature line still get no field, as before, so nothing is added there.

- The text is split by document first, then into halves at the page markers.
