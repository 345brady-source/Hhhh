## Summary

Document C is a ten-page form. It prints 138 places a person completes: 43 tick boxes, 14 places for initials, 68 blanks, 7 signatures and 6 dates beside a signature. 71 of the 138 are empty ruled cells (a box drawn with four rules) whose caption is printed above the box, in small capitals, without a colon. That one convention is what the reader does not yet read, and it explains 53 of the 54 places that get no field today.

The lower layer (`assets/js/anchors.js`) already finds every one of these cells correctly, except four signature cells that have a small printed "sign here" mark inside. The loss happens in `assets/js/placement.js`: twenty shallow cells are thrown away because the caption above them is taken for words inside them; the rest are dropped because a caption over a cell is only accepted when it is one of a few known kinds or ends in a colon; several are given the caption of the row below. Nothing is said about the places that were dropped: the read summary reports that every place got a field.

The second group of faults is about who completes a place. Captions that name a second investor or a kind of investor are treated as headings for everything under them, so ordinary investor places become the co-investor's or become "entities only"; the two sections that a custodian and an adviser complete are given to the investor as required fields; the two lists of initials (one for individuals, one for entities) are not told apart and all thirteen are required, although the form says to initial only the ones that apply.

Two trials on a scratch copy (repository untouched) prove the main causes: with four small changes 137 of 138 places get a field, with zero strays and zero overlaps, and the owner's first agreement stays at 47 fields with none marked.

**Primary recommendation:** Teach `placement.js` one new convention, "an empty ruled cell is named by the caption over it", guarded so that an ordinary table's empty cells stay without fields; fix the four smaller place-finding classes; then fix "who completes it" by making a caption speak only for its own place and by reading third-party sections and the individual and entity lists; make the read summary count what was read and not placed. Pin the field counts of the public documents in the regression check before any of this is built.

### Class 1: a shallow ruled cell with its caption just above (20 places, page 2)

**Cause.** [VERIFIED: `assets/js/placement.js:325-326`, read this session] The line is:

`const filled = (r) => r.table && r.clear < 12 && lines.some((ln) => ln.parts.some((p) => p.t === 'text' && p.y < r.y - 2 && p.y > r.y - r.clear - p.h && overlap(p.x0, p.x1, r.x0, r.x1) > 0));`

For a cell, `r.clear` is the cell's inner height. The window `p.y > r.y - r.clear - p.h` reaches one font height above the cell's top edge, so the caption printed over a cell that is 10 to 11 pt tall counts as "words in the cell", and the cell is removed at line 326 before any rule can name it. Cells 13 pt tall or more escape only because of `r.clear < 12`. `anchors.js` has already checked that the cell is empty before reporting it [VERIFIED: `assets/js/anchors.js:487`].

**Fix.** `filled()` does not apply to a rule with `cell: true`. Trial: all twenty reach the naming stage. **Not fitted to one document:** any form whose boxes are under 12 pt tall.

### Class 2: the caption over a cell (28 missed, 5 misnamed)

**Cause.** [VERIFIED: `assets/js/placement.js:833-860, 862-922, 1004-1019`, read this session]
- Step 2b names a cell only from words in the cell to its left in the row.
- Step 3 treats a cell like a ruled line: a label above counts only when `known()` recognises its kind or `labelOver()` finds a colon; a stack of blanks is read as "captions under" or "labels above" by counting known captions (`const caps = (nCap > 0 || splitCaps) && (nCap > nTop || ...)`). On a form whose rows are stacked tightly, the caption of the next row sits right under the cell above, so three cells on page 3 and two on page 4 take the caption of the row below (one becomes a signature for that reason).
- Step 5b then skips every cell still unnamed without a word: `if (sl.cell || sl.table || prose || pageNo || filledIn || ruledOff || sl.x1 - sl.x0 >= 0.85 * (rightEdge - leftEdge)) return (sl.skip = true);`
- A caption of two or three lines (pages 7 and 10) is read by its last line only.

**Fix.** One new step in `pageFields()`, before step 3: *an empty ruled cell is named by the caption over its top edge, all its lines.* The order of evidence is the one the picture reader already uses for boxes ("the words just before it on its row, or over it, or printed in it, or under it" [VERIFIED: `assets/js/placement.js:1228-1237`]), so both readers agree. Details the trials showed are needed:
- The caption is the text whose lowest line ends within about a line of the cell's top edge and whose centre lies over the cell; lines above it join while they are within 1.45 line heights, are the same size, and no other cell or rule lies between; three lines at most. A heading in another size or weight is not part of a caption (trial 1 swallowed a section heading into two captions).
- A known kind (city, state, postal code, phone, email, address, date, signature, tax number) gives that kind; any other caption gives a text field named by the caption. Required only when the caption itself says so, optional when it says "if applicable" or "optional".
- A caption centred over two cells of one row names both (page 10: two signature boxes under one caption).
- Sure, not marked, when the caption stands directly over its own cell.
- A cell with no caption gets no field, as today (see Risks: this is what keeps ordinary tables clean).

**Guard, required.** The caption must stand on open paper. Text that is itself inside a ruled cell of a grid is table content, not a form caption; and a caption must look like a label (a few words, no bullet, no sentence, not an amount). Without the guard, trial 1 added 13 fields to a public memorandum; with the first half of the guard, 2 remained (see Risks).

**Not fitted to one document:** "caption over an empty box" is the commonest way a printed form shows a blank; the picture reader already reads it this way.

### Class 3: caption in the left column, choices in the right column between (1 place, page 4)

**Cause.** The caption of a full-width cell is 28 pt above it at the cell's left edge; a block of tick boxes fills the right column in between. Today the cell takes the caption of the row below. With captions read from above (trial 2) it finds none within a line and gets no field, and the unused caption then acts as a heading (class 7). [VERIFIED: `C/run-try2.log`]

**Fix.** The search for a caption goes up from the cell's left edge as far as 46 pt when nothing is printed in the caption's own x-range between it and the cell. **General:** two-column form rows.

### Class 4: caption at the foot of one page, its cell at the top of the next (1 place, page 3)

**Cause.** The last body line of page 2 is a label that ends in a colon with no blank after it; the first thing on page 3 is an empty cell with no caption. No rule connects them.

**Fix.** Trial 2: remember a page's last body line when it ends in a colon and has no blank; give it to the first place of the next page when that place is an uncaptioned cell with nothing but the running header above it. The trial found the place and kept everything else. A carry of this kind already exists for headings (`c.carry`) [VERIFIED: `assets/js/placement.js:1086-1089`].

**About the Word file:** Word's own last layout, recorded in the file, breaks the page before this caption, so in a PDF saved by Word the caption most likely sits over its box on page 3 [ASSUMED: Word was not available to make that PDF]. The class is still general: any long form can break between a label and its box.

### Class 5: a signature cell with a printed "sign here" mark (4 missed, 3 neighbours wrong, page 10)

**Cause.** [VERIFIED: `assets/js/anchors.js:487`] `if (ink.some((g) => (g.x0 + g.x1) / 2 > a && (g.x0 + g.x1) / 2 < b && g.y > top && g.y - 0.4 * g.h < l.y + 1)) continue;` Any ink in a cell means "already answered", so a cell with one small mark at its left edge is no cell. The mark is then the nearest words to the left of the next empty cell in the row, and a bare mark of that kind is a signature label [VERIFIED: `assets/js/placement.js:158`, `SIG_RE` begins `/^(by|signed|sign|signature|sign here|please sign|x|`], so a date cell and a text cell become signatures. One of them is why the summary reports an investor signature on page 10.

**Fix.** In `anchors.js`: a single such mark at the left edge of an otherwise empty cell does not fill it; the cell is reported with the mark as its own. In `placement.js`: a mark inside a cell speaks for that cell only (it says "a signature goes here"), never for its neighbour. Trial: the four cells are read.

### Class 6: underscores printed just above another thing's rule (1 place, page 2)

**Cause.** [VERIFIED: `assets/js/anchors.js:427`] `const m = lines.find((l) => !l.frame && !p.frame && Math.abs(l.y - p.y) <= 2.2 && p.x0 - l.x1 <= 3.5 && p.x1 >= l.x0 - 3.5);` A 100 pt run of underscores that ends a caption row lies 0.8 pt above the 545 pt top edge of the cell below, so the two are joined into one line and the blank is lost. After un-joining (trial), the blank is read by `anchors.js` but carries `table: true` because one corner of the neighbouring cell touches it (`table: walls.length > 0`, line 516), and `placement.js` then drops a blank "in a table" whose label has no colon (line 771).

**Fix.** Underscores join a drawn rule only when the rule is their own underline (same extent within about 6 pt): that keeps the underlined runs on pages 1 and 5. `table` is set for a typed blank only when two walls cross it.

### Class 7: a caption that names its own place is not a heading (9 owner differences today)

**Cause.** [VERIFIED: `assets/js/placement.js:1069-1097`, read this session] A short line in capitals that names a second signer becomes a "skip" heading before the test whether the line was used as a label: `if (SKIP_HARD.test(t) && n <= 6 && (colon || (/[A-Z]{2}/.test(shout) && shout === shout.toUpperCase())) && !(next && next.t === 'slot')) return heads.push({ y: seg.y, x0: seg.x0, x1: seg.x1, who: '', skip: true, co: CO_INVESTOR.test(t), seg });` A heading governs everything under it in its x-range to the end of the page (`governs()`), so a caption for one second-investor cell turns a tick box and a blank 40 pt below into the co-investor's. The same happens with the kind of investor: a caption that contains a word for entities, or the word for an individual used of a payee, becomes a heading with `forType`, and three tick boxes become "entities only" and four places "individuals only". Once the cells are read (trial), the spread grows to 16 places, so this must be fixed together with class 2.

**Fix.** A line that has been taken as the caption of a place speaks for that place only. A heading is a free-standing line. A heading's reach ends at the next section rule or heading. In `coInvestor()` and `second()`, the kind comes from the label's known kind (a tax number stays a tax number; a date of birth is a blank to type, never a signing date): today a second signer's tax number becomes a signature and a date of birth becomes a date [VERIFIED: trial output; `assets/js/placement.js:525-529, 282-288`].

### Class 8: sections a third party completes (30 places)

**Cause.** [VERIFIED: `assets/js/placement.js:153-156, 1239`] The text reader knows third parties from two lists that differ: `SKIP_SOFT = /\b(custodian|broker|dealer|advis[eo]r|representative of|purchaser representative|registered representative|preparer|attorney|counsel|medallion)\b/` and, for pictures, `OTHERS_RE = /\bcustodian|\bbroker|\bdealer\b|\bregistered rep|\brepresentative|\badvis[eo]r|\bmanager\b|\bria\b|\btransfer agent|\bnotary|\bwitness/i`. The text reader's heading rule uses only `SKIP_HARD`, which has neither custodian nor adviser. There is no rule for a section whose heading says who completes it. Result today: of the 30 third-party places, 1 is treated as a third party's, 12 are the investor's (9 of them required and prefilled from the investor's own city, state, postal code, phone and email), 17 are missed.

**Fix.** One list of third-party words for both readers. A section heading that says a third party completes it governs every place to the next section rule or heading, or the page end, and carries to the next page while that page continues the section. A date or blank on the same row as a third party's signature is that party's. The result is the one the picture reader already gives: optional, nobody's, with the sentence `WHY_OTHERS` [VERIFIED: `assets/js/placement.js:1240`].

**Note for D-10.** This is not cosmetic: today the adviser's contact cells would arrive prefilled with the investor's own address and phone.

### Class 9: two lists of initials for two kinds of investor (13 places, page 5)

**Cause.** [VERIFIED: `assets/js/placement.js:1046-1047`] `const n = wordsOf(t).length; if (!t || n > 12) return;` The lead-in of each list is a sentence of thirteen words or more that ends in a colon, so it is never a heading, and all thirteen places are for every investor.

**Fix.** A lead-in sentence that ends in a colon, opens a lettered list and says which kind of investor the list is for ("if" plus the words `HEAD_PERSON` and `HEAD_ENTITY` already hold) governs the items of its list until the next lead-in at the same level. **General:** the standard accredited-investor questionnaire.

### Class 10: "initial the ones that apply" in a wording the reader does not know (13 places required instead of optional)

**Cause.** [VERIFIED: `assets/js/placement.js:578`] `const some = /\b(that appl(y|ies)|as applicable|if applicable|one of|at least one|any of|each that|all that apply|which appl(y|ies))\b/.test(t);` The form says the same thing with a synonym of "applicable" that is not in the list (its exact words are in `C/class-notes.md`), so each of the thirteen initials is required [VERIFIED: `C/out/report.txt`, required differences].

**Why it matters.** An investor could not finish without initialling statements that are not true of them. This blocks D-10 for every kind of investor.

**Fix.** Widen the list to the wording class, not to one phrase: each phrase it holds with "applicable" also with its common synonyms ("appropriate", "relevant"), and "whichever applies". A list under a conditional lead-in (class 9) is optional item by item in any case.

### Class 11: a list mark without its bracket (1 place, page 5)

**Cause.** [VERIFIED: `assets/js/placement.js:121`] `const LIST_MARK = /^(\(?(\d{1,2}|[a-z]|[ivx]{1,4})[.)]|[•·▪■○●*–-])$/i;` One item's letter is printed without its bracket. The blank after it is then "a blank inside a sentence": a text field named by the bare letter, marked.

**Fix.** A blank with the same left edge and width as three or more initials blanks of the same list is one of them. This does not depend on the mark at all, so a misprint cannot break it.

### Class 12: tick boxes in several columns; a box whose label is in another size (6 boxes, page 3)

**Cause.** [VERIFIED by reading `assets/js/placement.js:102-108, 400-434` and by the probe `C/out/probe.json`; not proved by trial] Printed lines are built across the whole page by baseline. The pass "Drawn squares that stand on this line" (`if (mid > ln.y + 0.35 * ln.h || mid < ln.y - 1.1 * ln.h) return; b.line = ln;`) also takes box characters, and it runs line by line from the top: a line of small print in another column, 4 pt higher, claims a box before the box's own line is reached. The box then has no words beside it, and its words go to the next box that is claimed late. Five boxes end up marked "no words beside it" and one carries a neighbour's words.

**Fix.** A box character belongs to the line of its own glyph; bind all box characters first, in one pass, before drawn squares are assigned. A drawn square goes to the line whose words begin just right of it. **General:** any table of choices in columns.

### Class 13: an acceptance block that names someone other than the firm (2 places, page 10)

**Cause.** [VERIFIED: `assets/js/placement.js:137`] `FIRM_WORDS = /\baccept(ed|ance)\b|\bgeneral partner\b|\bmanaging (member|partner)\b|\bmanager\b|\bsponsor\b|\bissuer\b|\bcountersign|\bfor the (company|fund|partnership|issuer)\b/` makes the block the firm's without doubt, while its caption also carries a third-party word (a dealer).

**Fix.** When one caption carries both a firm word and a third-party word, the place is the firm's and is marked, with a sentence that says why. This is the only review mark the answer sheet expects outside the third-party sections. The owner is asked (Open questions).

### Class 14: the read summary (D-08)

**Cause.** [VERIFIED: `assets/js/placement.js:1420-1423`] `res.places = out.length;` counts proposals, and `left` lists only pages where a proposal lost an overlap. A printed place that `anchors.js` read and no rule named is never counted. Signature lines are counted as `p.type === 'signature' || p.type === 'initials'` [VERIFIED: `assets/js/esign.js:2924`], so thirteen initials are reported as signature lines.

**Fix.** Count what `anchors.js` read (cells, lines, boxes) against what got a field, and say so when a captioned cell or a line has none. Count initials apart from signatures. Say when places are for a second investor or for a third party, and that nobody is asked to complete the third party's. Say when the only acceptance block is in doubt.

### Class 15: sure in fact, marked today (4 marks, 1 wrong label)

## Common Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: fixing the cells and stopping
**What goes wrong:** With only the place-finding fixes, 136 of 138 places have a field but owner differences rise from 34 to 56, because every newly read cell under a caption that names a second signer or a kind of investor inherits it.
**How to avoid:** Plan classes 1 to 6 and classes 7 to 9 as one unit of work, scored together.

### Pitfall 2: trusting "zero unclean"
**What goes wrong:** Wrong fields on empty table cells pass the judge.
**How to avoid:** Pin field counts per public document in the first plan, before any reader change.

### Pitfall 3: required fields nobody can honestly complete
**What goes wrong:** Initials for statements that do not apply, a custodian's name for an investor with no custodian, an adviser's phone prefilled with the investor's.
**How to avoid:** The end-to-end walk for an individual and for an entity (D-10) is part of this document's proof, not an extra.

### Pitfall 4: a caption growing into a heading
**What goes wrong:** Trial 1 joined a section heading to the caption under it.
**How to avoid:** Caption lines share one size and weight; stop at any change.

### Pitfall 5: text from the document in committed files
**What goes wrong:** A test or a comment quotes a caption.
**How to avoid:** Tests for these classes use made-up captions on generated pages; the scorer and answer sheet stay in scratch.
