                     Article I – Definitions & General Provisions
         1. Definitions

For the purposes of this Agreement, unless otherwise expressly indicated herein, the following terms shall
have the following meanings:
         AFE. Also known as “Authority for Expenditure.” An AFE describes the estimated costs
associated with a specific oilfield operation, normally the drilling and completing of a new Well. In general,
an operator who proposes to drill a new Well will prepare the AFE and submit it for approval to other
owners of a Working Interest (Non-Operators) in the Well. Upon receipt of an AFE, a Non-Operator
generally has the ability to consent to the proposed operation and pay its proportionate share of costs within
a specified time period or not consent to the proposed operation and either be penalized or lose its interest
in the Well.
       Adjusted Capital Account Deficit. With respect to any Unit Holder, the deficit balance, if any, in
such Unit Holder’s Capital Account as of the end of the relevant tax year, after giving effect to the
adjustments set forth herein or the Code or Regulations as determined by the Managing Partner.
         Affiliate. Any Person controlling, controlled by, or under common control with another Person.
        Assumed Marginal Tax Rate. Thirty-seven percent (37%) regardless of a particular Unit Holder’s
actual highest individual or aggregate marginal tax rate at the federal, state, local, and/or foreign level.
         Book Depreciation. For any asset for any fiscal period, an amount equal to the depreciation,
amortization or other cost recovery deduction allowable for federal income tax purposes with respect to an
asset for which the Gross Asset Value equals its adjusted tax basis. If the Gross Asset Value of an asset
differs from its adjusted basis for federal income tax purposes at the beginning of such fiscal period, Book
Depreciation shall be an amount that bears the same ratio to the Gross Asset Value of that asset at the

deduction allowable for that asset for such period bears to the adjusted tax basis of that asset at the beginning

oil and gas properties), or other cost recovery deduction allowable for any asset for such period is zero, then
Book Depreciation for that asset shall be determined with reference to such beginning Gross Asset Value

         Capital Account. The account maintained for a Unit Holder as described herein.
        Class A Unit. A Unit of Partnership Interest designated as Class A. While the Managing Partner

         Class H2 Unit Holder. A Person holding Class H2 Units.

        Class Sharing Ratio. The ratio resulting from dividing the Units of a specific class held by a Unit
Holder by all outstanding Units of that same class. For example, if 100 Class A Units are issued, a Class A
Unit Holder holding one (1) Class A Unit would have a Class Sharing Ratio of 1 divided by 100 Units, or
0.01. The Class Sharing Ratio shall initially be determined as of the end of the offering of Units and
thereafter as necessary.

abandoning any Well before a completion attempt. IDCs shall also include, for all purposes under this

Amounts. When the offering of Units in the Partnership is closed, the Management Fee will not be deducted
from the Subscription Amounts of the Partners but will be paid by the Partnership quarterly in arrears from
funds which would otherwise be available for distribution to the Partners herein. To the extent that the
Partnership has insufficient distributable funds during a particular quarter to fully pay the Management Fee
payable during such quarter, then the amount of such unpaid Management Fee will be carried forward and
payable in the succeeding quarter(s) until fully paid. The Management Fee shall terminate and be of no
further force or effect beginning with the quarter after all Unit Holders have achieved Payback. For
example, if the Unit Holders achieve Payback in Q1 of a Fiscal Year, no Management Fee shall be payable
beginning in Q2 of such Fiscal Year.

         Net Operating Cash Flow. Net operating cash flow for Partnership distribution purposes is at the

         Operating Agreement. Also referred to as a “Joint Operating Agreement” or “JOA.” An
Operating Agreement covers a particular prospect or contract area and outlines and defines the rights,
responsibilities, risks, and penalties between two or more parties involved in exploring, developing, and

and which parties will be Non-Operators. Oftentimes, an Operating Agreement will require a test Well be
drilled within a certain time period and as to a certain depth and will contain certain penalties in the event
a Non-Operator does not consent to proposed operations. Model Form Operating Agreements promulgated
by the American Association of Professional Landmen are widely used in the industry.
